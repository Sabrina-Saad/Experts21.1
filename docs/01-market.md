# 01 — Market: Americans Traveling Abroad

> **Scope:** Demand-side sizing for a "first steps abroad" travel platform serving U.S. residents (flight deals/fare tracking, trip planning, language prep, mainstream + off-the-beaten-path destinations, hand-holding guidance).
> **Research date:** November 2026. Figures marked *(verify)* came from secondary sources and should be confirmed before use in investor materials.

## What the numbers mean for us

1. **Outbound demand is strong and still growing**, even while *inbound* visits to the U.S. fell in 2025. Americans kept going abroad through a year of economic and political noise — the core demand is resilient.
2. **The "not yet traveled" pool is enormous.** Roughly half of Americans have no valid passport. Every year ~27M passports are issued, and a meaningful slice are first-time adult applicants — that is the top of our funnel, and it is measurable (passport application volume is published by State Dept).
3. **The booking layer is saturated; the confidence layer is not.** $100B+ flows through OTAs, dominated by Expedia and Booking Holdings. Competing on *booking* is a losing game for a startup. Competing on *readiness, confidence and guidance* — then monetizing the booking via affiliates — is open space.
4. **Market is plateauing at the OTA level** (Phocuswright, 2025), which pushes incumbents toward loyalty, B2B and AI agents. Growth for a newcomer comes from expanding the pool of travelers (converting non-travelers), not stealing share.
5. **Long trips (~16 days avg overseas)** mean high-value bookings per trip and many decision points (flights, lodging, rail, eSIM, insurance, tours) — each a monetization slot.

## Market sizing (working model — for the finance agent to refine)

| Layer | Definition | Estimate | Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | All U.S. adults who travel internationally by air or could within 3 years | ~100M+ people | ~48% passport holders (~130M+ adults incl. cards) plus new applicants |
| **SAM** | U.S. adults planning a first or early (1–3 trips lifetime) overseas trip in the next 24 months who plan online | ~15–25M people | Assumption: 15–20% of passport holders are novice/infrequent overseas travelers + ~5M+ first-time adult passport applicants/yr *(assumption — validate with survey)* |
| **SOM (yr 3)** | Realistically reachable with a seed-stage budget | 150K–400K active users; 15K–40K paying | 1–2% of SAM reached; 8–12% convert to paid |

**Revenue per engaged user (illustrative):** If a first-time traveler books one ~$1,200 flight + 10 nights of lodging + insurance + eSIM through affiliate links, blended affiliate revenue is roughly **$60–$150 per trip** (lodging 4–6% commission, insurance 15–30%, eSIM 10–20%, tours 8%, flights ~$0–10 via metasearch CPC). Add a subscription ($5–$10/mo or $49–$99/yr) for the guidance + fare alert product.

## Demand drivers

- **Passport surge** post-2022; record issuance years in FY2023–FY2025.
- **Social media inspiration** (TikTok/Instagram) creates aspiration among people with zero travel experience — but also "travel dysmorphia"/anxiety (see `02-target-customers.md`).
- **Strong-dollar periods** make Europe, Japan, Latin America more accessible *(currency is cyclical — a risk too)*.
- **Remote/hybrid work** extends trip lengths ("work from abroad").
- **AI trip planning** is normalizing — users now expect conversational planning.

## Demand headwinds

- Affordability is the #1 barrier for first-timers (EF Ultimate Break/Qualtrics Dec 2025; AARP for 50+).
- New entry formalities raise perceived complexity: **UK ETA (£16)**, **EU EES** biometrics (operational since April 2026), **EU ETIAS (€20)** — ETIAS launch date repeatedly postponed, no firm date as of late 2026.
- Safety perceptions, geopolitics, State Dept advisories.
- Airfare volatility and airline capacity constraints.

> **Opportunity framing:** Each new formality (ETA, ETIAS, EES) is *friction for incumbents and content for us* — a clear, calm "here's exactly what to do" checklist is the product.
